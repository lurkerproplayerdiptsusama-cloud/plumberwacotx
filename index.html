Common Causes of Low Water Pressure in Your Home

April 26, 2025   |   7 min read

Low water pressure can make everyday tasks like showering, washing dishes, and doing laundry frustrating. If you have noticed weak water flow from your faucets or showerhead, understanding the possible causes can help you identify the problem. 1. Partially Closed Water Valves

A valve that is not fully open can restrict water flow to your fixtures. Check the shutoff valves under sinks and near toilets, as well as the main water supply valve. If a valve is stuck or corroded, avoid forcing it because this could cause damage or leaks.

2. Mineral Buildup in Faucets and Showerheads

Hard water minerals can accumulate inside faucet aerators and showerheads over time. This buildup can narrow the openings and reduce water flow. If only one fixture is affected, cleaning the aerator or showerhead may help restore normal pressure.

3. Leaking or Deteriorating Pipes

Damaged pipes can allow water to escape before it reaches your fixtures. Watch for damp spots, water stains, unexplained increases in water bills, or the sound of running water when taps are off. Older metal pipes may also develop internal corrosion that restricts flow.

Quick Tip

Notice whether low pressure affects one fixture, several fixtures, or the entire house. This can help narrow down whether the cause is a local blockage or a larger plumbing issue.

4. Problems With the Main Water Supply

Sometimes the issue is outside your home. Utility maintenance, supply interruptions, or changes in the local water system can temporarily affect pressure. If nearby homes are experiencing the same issue, contact your water provider for more information.

5. Pressure Regulator Issues

Some homes use a pressure-reducing valve to control incoming water pressure. If this component malfunctions, water pressure may become inconsistent or unusually low. A qualified professional can assess the regulator and determine whether adjustment or replacement is needed.

When to Call a Professional

If low pressure persists, affects multiple fixtures, or appears alongside leaks and unusual pipe noises, further inspection may be necessary. Identifying the cause early can help prevent more serious plumbing damage.

Final Thoughts

Low water pressure can result from simple blockages, partially closed valves, damaged pipes, or supply problems. Paying attention to when and where the pressure drops can help you identify the likely cause and determine the next step.
